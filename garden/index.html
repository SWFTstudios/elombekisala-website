    How to read a tree

    Real branches, nothing invented.

    13 of the 19 projects have a public repository. Their trees are read straight from git: 224 branches in all. The project name seeds the rest, so a tree looks the same on every visit.

    Trunk
    The default branch. Short spurs along its top carry its own foliage, more of them the more commits it holds.
    Limbs
    One per branch, leaving the trunk at the height where the branch forked off. Longer and thicker for branches with more commits of their own.
    Mature foliage
    Merged branches: the work is part of the project now.
    Fresh growth
    Open branches with recent commits, tinted toward the project’s color.
    Deadwood (jin)
    Open branches that went quiet for more than 45 days. Bonsai artists keep deadwood on purpose, too.
    Shape
    Informal upright (moyōgi) for Web app; Twin trunk (sōkan) for Automation; Windswept (fukinagashi) for iOS app; Cascade (kengai) for Shopify / e-commerce first; Broom (hōkidachi) for Brand site; Literati (bunjin) for AI; Formal upright (chokkan) for Webflow. Projects without a public repo grow one limb per tool in their stack.
